# Prologue — draft v2

> Status: **draft for review.** It reworks the unfinished prologue from GDD v1. The v1 text switched between first and third person; v2 uses **close third person, past tense** throughout. A short cold open is kept in present tense on purpose: it's a flash-forward.
> In game, this plays as **text over storybook panels** (cold open), then a **playable walk** (Max's morning) with an inner-monologue VO.

## Cold open (flash-forward, present tense)

A shriek rolls through the mist on the other side of the door, and every hair on Max's arms rises.

He has been fighting all day, or all night. Down here he can't tell, and it doesn't feel like a single day any more. Every step of it has been for the one thing he has left. So why are his legs refusing to move?

*SMACK.*

His own shaking palm cracks against his thigh, and the sound echoes around the test chamber. His legs wake up.

He opens the door.

## Part 1 — "Luck"

They say failure builds character. That every fall is a stepping stone.

Max Hunt had stepped on a lot of stones. What nobody mentioned was luck, the part of the equation you don't get a say in. You could learn from every mistake you ever made and still lose to a wet road and a tired truck driver at 11:40 on a Tuesday night.

Two coins clinked in his pocket as he walked to work. The holes in his shoes had turned his feet into blocks of ice somewhere around the second block. Forty-five cents, enough for nothing, and he was smiling anyway.

Because there was a letter in his other pocket.

Five years ago, the crash had taken his parents and the state had taken his sister. *A specialist medical foster program*, they'd said. *It's better for her. Her condition.* Emilia had been ten. Max had been sixteen, old enough to argue and too young for anyone to listen.

The Hales had taken him in. Walter and June: kind, quiet, smelling of coffee and old wool. For a while it was almost a home. Then he'd made his mistakes, and then he'd made the one choice he didn't regret: standing in front of a judge and saying the drugs were his, so the man the Hales owed money to would leave them alone.

A year ago, the Hales had stopped looking at him. Then they'd stopped speaking to him. Then June had changed the locks.

Now there was the studio apartment over the laundromat. There was the front desk at Dell's Hardware, where Mr. Dell apologised every Friday for the size of the envelope. There was the stack of rejection emails: *after reviewing your background check…* And there was the cotton-ball bruise in the crook of his elbow, from the blood-donation van that had paid him three hundred dollars last month. Three hundred dollars, gone in a week. The van had been parked outside the store again this morning. The nurse waved at him like they were old friends.

And there was the letter.

## Part 2 — The letter

> *(In game: an examinable item, read aloud in Emilia's VO. Huckley's handwritten note is clipped to the back.)*

**Dear Big Brother,**

Today is finally the day! The doctors say the testing is all done and I get to go home. Home with **you**. I still can't believe it.

I'm sorry I disappeared. I know it wasn't my fault (Dr. Lorraine says that a lot), but I'm still sorry you had to be sad on your own after Mom and Dad. I was sad too. I tried to be brave like you. Mostly I was.

I've drawn you a hundred pictures. Jade said that was too many but I don't think there's such a thing. Some of them are of us at the lake. Some of them are of the house we'll have. It has a room just for drawing and a door that doesn't lock.

The others went home already. Vicky, Jasper and Jade. Nobody said goodbye, but that's okay, I'll find them after. Dr. Mán says I'm the most special one, so I had to stay the longest. He says tonight there's one last medicine and then I'm done. It's a little bit scary but it's the **last** one!

Please come get me. Don't be late like you always were for school.

I love you more than the sky.

**— Em**

*P.S. Do you still hum the song Mom used to sing? I hum it all the time so I don't forget it. Sometimes the lights flicker when I do. Isn't that funny?*

## Part 3 — Six days (playable montage)

Max goes to St. Brigid's on the 14th. Nobody comes.

He goes back on the 15th, the 16th, the 17th. Huckley's number rings out. On the sixth night he notices fresh tyre tracks leading *behind* the boarded-up clinic, into a loading bay that shouldn't still have power.

The door is open, just a crack. A child's drawing is taped to it at eye level: two stick figures holding hands, one tall, one small.

*Control returns to the player.*

### Writer's notes
- The P.S. sets up three things: Emilia's lullaby motif (music and puzzle solutions), her powers (the flickering lights), and her innocence about what the "last medicine" was.
- "The others went home already" plants the reveal about the Hollow Children.
- The Hales' betrayal stays a mystery until the Security Hub files in Act 3.
- The donation van (Halvorsen Biologics) is a LANTERN front. Its return pays off in Act 3, when Max finds his own intake file.
